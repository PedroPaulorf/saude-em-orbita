SECTION 02

High-level project summary

Health7 is a functional prototype designed to support preventive health monitoring during long-duration space missions. The system brings together physical check-ups, symptom reports, behavioral check-ins, and cognitive tests. Instead of interpreting each measurement in isolation, Health7 compares current records with the astronaut's personal pre-mission baseline and recent mission history.

An explicit rule-based engine identifies stable conditions, relevant changes, persistent patterns, and signs that may require medical evaluation. The platform then shows what changed, which records contributed to the result, and what follow-up actions should be considered.

Artificial intelligence is used as an explanation and communication layer. It translates selected monitoring information into clear language and helps organize a follow-up roadmap. It does not determine the monitoring state, diagnose a disease, prescribe treatment, or replace a physician.

Health7 addresses the challenge by transforming dispersed health indicators into understandable and actionable information. Its goal is to increase astronaut autonomy, support preventive action, and improve the quality of information available for medical evaluation when immediate assistance from Earth may be limited.

Evidence boundary

The current version is a software prototype demonstrated with synthetic crew scenarios and selected NASA data and research resources. It has not been clinically validated or deployed in spaceflight.

Core value proposition

Health7 converts fragmented health records into an explainable personal trajectory and a practical next step before an overt clinical event.

SECTION 03

Problem, solution, and user journey

The problem

During long-duration missions, astronauts are exposed to isolation, altered gravity, radiation, confined environments, disrupted sleep, and communication delays. Health measurements may come from different tests, devices, and self-reported observations. A single value may not explain whether a meaningful personal change is occurring, while delayed communication can limit immediate access to specialists.

The solution

Health7 organizes physical measurements, symptoms, behavioral check-ins, cognitive tests, and historical records in one monitoring environment. It compares recent information with the astronaut's individual baseline, identifies patterns through explicit rules, and presents the evidence together with a follow-up roadmap.

Example user journey

After several monitoring days, Health7 may identify that a measurement or cognitive result changed repeatedly compared with the astronaut's baseline. The system does not announce a disease. It shows the changed records, explains whether the pattern is isolated or persistent, recommends what to check next, and indicates when medical evaluation should be requested.

SECTION 04

Benefits, technology, and validation status

Expected benefits

  • Greater astronaut autonomy during long missions.
  • Earlier recognition of relevant health changes.
  • Clear explanations instead of unexplained alerts.
  • Personalized comparison with each astronaut's baseline.
  • Better organization of physical, behavioral, and cognitive records.
  • A traceable summary for later medical review.
  • Practical follow-up actions based on monitoring states.
  • Reduced dependence on continuous communication with Earth.

Technology

LayerTechnology and purpose

Decision logicExplicit demonstration rules separated from generative AI

Current limitations

  • Crew profiles and daily physiological scenarios are synthetic or literature-informed synthetic data.
  • There has been no clinical or in-flight validation.
  • The prototype does not diagnose disease or calculate a validated disease probability.
  • Most measurements are entered manually or through simulated workflows.
  • Wearable imports are separate from the main evaluation flow, and no validated continuous sensor stream is active.
  • The system does not automatically contact a physician on Earth and is not fully offline.
  • NASA resources provide research and mission context; they do not represent NASA certification or endorsement.

Operational safeguard

The rule engine determines the monitoring state. AI cannot change that result. If the AI service is unavailable, the prototype can present a local fallback roadmap based on the current state and already available information.

SECTION 07

Partner data and other references

Terrestrial proxy dataset

The Multilevel Monitoring of Activity and Sleep in Healthy People dataset was studied as a terrestrial physiological proxy. It includes heart rate, RR intervals, actigraphy, sleep information, questionnaires, and saliva-related records. MMASH contains data from people on Earth and must not be described as astronaut data.

Background and comparative references

  • Canadian Space Agency Bio-Monitor.
  • European Space Agency EveryWear.
  • Royal College of Physicians NEWS2 framework.
  • NASA fatigue and sleep research resources.
  • Biomedical Results from Skylab.

These references helped the team understand existing monitoring technologies and interfaces. They are not presented as integrated partner datasets or as clinical validation of the prototype.

Software and content attribution

Known software resources include React, TypeScript, Vite, Node.js, Fastify, PostgreSQL, and n8n. Before final submission, the team must add the exact attribution and license for every external code library, icon, font, image, video, audio asset, dataset, text excerpt, and design component. Copyrighted material should only be included when permission is available.

Competitive positioning

Health7 does not claim to invent multimodal monitoring, the first integrated astronaut dashboard, or the first personalized health tool. Its proposed contribution is the combination of a personal trajectory, explainable monitoring states, and preventive next actions before an overt clinical event.
